**Applicant:** HY, University of Toronto Physics

**Scope:** Eligibility, curriculum analysis, selection scoring, documents, cost, applicant-specific strategy, and preparation schedule  
**Last verified:** 2026-09-04

## 1. Executive assessment

TUM AEP is the appropriate English-taught TUM Physics route for HY. Its high-energy-physics area explicitly includes computational physics, data analysis, and Monte Carlo methods, which can connect the UCL project with the MaNGA/PyMC background. The more directly named MSc Physics (Nuclear, Particle and Astrophysics), or KTA, is currently taught in German and is therefore not the practical default without sufficient German proficiency.

Admission is highly rule-based. TUM first checks whether the bachelor's curriculum is comparable to its research-oriented Physics BSc, then scores curriculum equivalence, grades, and a program-specific motivation letter. HY's research can improve the letter and interview, but it cannot replace missing core coursework.

| Decision factor | Assessment for HY | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Degree field | University of Toronto Physics | Correct field, subject to detailed equivalence review |
| Curriculum | Likely strong in theory and mathematics; laboratory, condensed-matter, and breadth requirements must be mapped | Curriculum analysis is the most important admission gate |

## 4. Application route and deadlines

Two linked applications are required:

1. **TUMonline:** the degree-program application and all admission documents.
2. **uni-assist:** the Preliminary Documentation, or VPD, for a master's entrance qualification earned outside a German university.

An application to uni-assist alone is not an application to TUM.

| Intended start | Application period | Planning note |
|---|---:|---|
| Winter semester | **1 January–31 May** | Complete by 15 January for the department's early-review route intended to reduce visa delay |
| Summer semester | **1 September–30 November** | Curriculum is optimized for a winter start |

The department states that a complete winter application filed by 15 January is normally discussed in February, with notification around mid-March after central document checks. Regular first-stage results are expected around early August. The closing dates are exclusion deadlines: all required documents other than a permitted pending VPD must be uploaded in time.

For Fall 2027, the working plan is to open the TUMonline application on 1 January 2027 and target **15 January 2027**, while treating **31 May 2027** as the final recurring deadline. Both dates must be rechecked against the live cycle.

## 5. Academic eligibility and curriculum gate

TUM requires a bachelor's degree in physics or an equivalent degree comparable in form and content to its research-oriented Physics BSc.

### 5.1 Reference curriculum

The formal aptitude regulations use these TUM subject groups:

| Subject group | TUM reference credits | Included areas |
|---|---:|---|
| Foundations of experimental physics | 34 | Mechanics, electromagnetism, optics, thermodynamics, atomic physics |
| Advanced experimental physics | 10 | Nuclear, particle and astrophysics; condensed-matter physics |
| Foundations of theoretical physics | 34 | Analytical mechanics, electrodynamics, quantum mechanics, thermodynamics, statistical physics |
| Mathematics | 32 | Linear algebra and analysis foundations |
| Laboratory courses | 21 | Experimental laboratory training |
| Bachelor's thesis | 12 | Scientific, foundational, and methods-oriented independent work |

TUM emphasizes theoretical physics, condensed-matter physics, nuclear/particle physics, and laboratory work because these areas are often absent from engineering or shorter degrees.

### 5.2 Missing competencies

The committee performs a competency-based review rather than a title-only module match. The regulations award up to 60 curriculum points and deduct for missing competencies. An applicant below 30 curriculum points is not admitted to the aptitude assessment. The department explains the same gate as rejection when missing competencies exceed 30 TUM credits.

Smaller gaps may lead to conditional admission with up to 30 credits of TUM bachelor's courses to be passed during the first year. These courses are generally taught and examined in German. A conditional offer can therefore create both workload and language risk.

### 5.3 Transcript progress at application

The formal rules request a transcript containing at least 130, 160, or 190 credits for a six-, seven-, or eight-semester bachelor's program, respectively, together with the underlying curriculum or module handbook. TUM's departmental guidance also describes provisional admission for an unfinished qualifying degree.

Because U of T uses its own credit system, HY should not self-convert credits as final evidence. The VPD and TUM curriculum-analysis form should be used, and the expected minimum progress for a four-year Canadian degree should be confirmed with the Physics academic advisor.

## 7. English and German requirements

### English

TUM's general accepted minimums include TOEFL iBT 88 or IELTS Academic 6.5. HY should normally use English-medium education instead:

- TUM generally accepts a bachelor's or master's program taught at least 50% in English, shown on the transcript/degree certificate or through an official university confirmation.
- The Physics department also states that at least 10 ECTS taught and examined in English is accepted when certified by the university.

An official U of T medium-of-instruction letter is the safest document. It should state that the degree is taught and examined in English.

### German

German is not an admission requirement for AEP. TUM nevertheless requires international students without German evidence to complete an integrative German-language module, normally at A1 level or above, within the first two semesters.

German also matters if conditional bachelor's modules are imposed, because those modules are usually taught and examined in German.

## 8. VPD and country-specific documents

uni-assist evaluates the foreign master's entrance qualification, converts grades and credits, and issues the VPD. It normally takes approximately four to six weeks, with extra time possible for questions.

Key rules:

- Apply for the TUM master's VPD; a VPD for another university is not accepted.
- The VPD can be requested before the TUM application period. If the intended semester is not open, TUM says the current semester can be selected.
- Apply to uni-assist no later than the program deadline and pay the processing fee.
- If the final VPD is pending, upload TUM's confirmation form by the application deadline.
- For a winter master's application, the current grace deadline for replacing the confirmation with the final VPD is **15 July**, provided all other admission documents were complete on time.

TUM's country-specific page lists APS requirements for qualifications from China, India, and Vietnam. APS applicability should be determined from the country of the relevant educational qualifications and the documents requested in TUMonline; nationality alone should not be used to infer the answer.

## 9. Selection scoring

### 9.1 Initial eligibility screen

- Complete and timely documents are mandatory.
- Curriculum equivalence is scored up to 60 points.
- Fewer than 30 curriculum points means rejection before the main aptitude scoring.

### 9.2 Stage 1: 100 points

| Component | Maximum | Rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Curriculum equivalence | 60 | Competency comparison with the TUM Physics BSc |
| Grades | 20 | One point for each 0.1 that the converted German average is better than 3.0, capped at 20 |
| Motivation letter | 20 | Program qualifications and admission requirements: 8; goals, specialization choices, academic connection, and TUM research fit: 12 |

For foreign degrees, TUM uses the converted German grade. If more than 130 ECTS are available, the applicant may list the best-graded modules totaling 130 ECTS. If the list is omitted, TUM uses the submitted overall average.

| Stage-1 score | Outcome |
|---:|---|
| 66 or more | Passes the aptitude assessment, subject to any curriculum conditions |
| 55–65 | Invited to Stage 2 interview |
| Below 55 | Rejected |

### 9.3 Stage 2 interview

The interview lasts 20–30 minutes and may be conducted by video conference when approved. It evaluates:

1. the applicant's assessment of their own fit and reasons for choosing AEP; and
2. foundational physics knowledge and the ability to outline solutions to representative physics problems.

The second criterion is weighted twice as strongly as the first. The interview contributes up to 45 points. These points are added to the curriculum and grade points from Stage 1; **80 points or more** are required to pass Stage 2.

## 10. Applicant-specific scoring implications

### Curriculum points are decisive

HY should build a defensible, evidence-based mapping of U of T courses to the six TUM subject groups. Research experience does not receive a separate numerical score and cannot replace missing credited foundations.

Likely review priorities are:

- whether the transcript contains enough upper-level theoretical physics;
- whether condensed-matter and nuclear/particle breadth are documented;
- whether laboratory credits approach TUM's expected depth;
- whether a credited research course, thesis, or equivalent can support the bachelor's-thesis competency; and
- whether official course descriptions show the necessary content rather than relying on abbreviated transcript titles.

### Grade points remain uncertain

Do not directly convert 3.0/4.0 into a German grade. uni-assist and TUM will use the official grading-scale information. HY should use TUM's best-130-ECTS option if eligible and strategically select the strongest relevant advanced modules, while preserving exact credit totals and grades.

### The motivation letter can add meaningful points

The letter is worth 20% of Stage 1. It should use the scoring rubric explicitly:

- **Qualification and requirements, up to 8 points:** show course coverage, computational skills, research methods, and awareness of any curriculum gap.
- **Goals and TUM-specific plan, up to 12 points:** name the AEP high-energy-physics area, relevant computational/data-analysis/Monte Carlo offerings, and TUM research groups that connect to the proposed direction.

| Section | Target length | Content |

| Academic eligibility | 120–150 words | Map the strongest theory, experiment, math, lab, and computing preparation to the TUM prerequisites |
| MaNGA research | 130–160 words | Bayesian model, PyMC 5, MCMC, uncertainty, data interpretation, manuscript status |
| UCL research | 120–150 words | Exact computational particle problem, numerical method, individual contribution, validation, output |
| Why AEP at TUM | 150–190 words | High-energy specialization, computational/data-analysis/Monte Carlo content, named research environment, and intended research phase |
| Goal | 40–60 words | Research master's training leading toward computational particle/astroparticle or dark-matter PhD work |

The letter must show why **AEP**, rather than only why TUM or physics generally. The statement should connect each proposed specialization to prior preparation and a realistic research objective.
