## 1. Decision summary

The U of T Physics MSc is a worthwhile application because the research fit is strong and a current U of T student can still create new, high-value evidence before the deadline. It should not be treated as a safe option. With a 3.0/4.0 cumulative GPA, the application is a **Reach** unless the final-year or upper-year record is clearly stronger.

The most valuable use of home-university access is to begin a real supervised project this term and earn a detailed U of T faculty reference. Merely introducing oneself to faculty or obtaining a friendly reply is unlikely to carry comparable weight because the Graduate Admission Committee states that it decides admission from the written application and supporting documents.

## 2. Formal eligibility and the same-university question

No official restriction was found on a U of T Physics undergraduate applying to the U of T Physics MSc. The published eligibility rule accepts an appropriate bachelor's degree in Physics or a comparably rigorous field and does not exclude degrees from U of T.

There is also no published preference, reserved quota, automatic continuation, or guaranteed admission for internal applicants. The Graduate Admission Committee makes the decision, and the department lists supervision capacity, funding, cohort size, and the overall strength of the applicant pool as factors.

The internal-student benefits that can be supported by official information are:

1. The U of T degree is taught and examined in English, so an English facility test is not required; a confirming letter may still be needed unless the transcript contains the appropriate notation.
2. A Physics Major, Specialist, or Joint Specialist student with the normal 2.7 cumulative-GPA threshold can arrange a PHY37x/PHY47x supervised study or research-project course with a U of T faculty member.
3. The applicant can contact and meet local faculty, attend group activities, and produce a recent research report or project result before admission decisions.
4. A U of T research supervisor can write a locally informed reference. The departmental FAQ specifically says that a letter from a professor who supervised a small final-year project is more informative than one from the instructor of a large first-year course.

Being enrolled at U of T does not convert an international applicant into a domestic applicant. If HY is not a Canadian citizen or permanent resident, the international-applicant competition remains relevant.

## 4. Applicant assessment

### Strengths

- The MaNGA project provides a coherent dark-matter research question and concrete Bayesian methods: PyMC 5, MCMC, priors, posteriors, convergence checks, and uncertainty analysis.
- The UCL project adds computational particle physics under one-to-one supervision and may provide the strongest research reference.
- The combination of astrophysical dark-matter inference and particle-physics computation fits several U of T groups without requiring an artificial change of direction.
- A first-author manuscript, once uploaded to arXiv, would give the committee a verifiable research output.
- Current access to U of T faculty makes it possible to add a department-based project and reference before or during the application cycle.

### Risks

- A 3.0 cumulative GPA is below the department's published B+ wording and only near the SGS mid-B wording. The final-year or relevant upper-year average must be calculated from the transcript rather than inferred from cumulative GPA.
- The department reports about 350 applications and 35–50 entering graduate students across MSc and PhD programs. These data show high selectivity but cannot be converted into an MSc acceptance rate.
- The department admits only a small number of international applicants. A U of T bachelor's degree removes the language-test issue but not the international funding and capacity constraint.
- The MaNGA project arose through a commercial class-project arrangement, so its recommendation letter may be less persuasive than the research product itself.
- Two references only means every letter must contain detailed evidence of research readiness.

## 5. Faculty contact strategy for Fall 2026

### Priority 1 — Ziqing Hong

Prof. Hong works on SuperCDMS dark-matter direct detection and the Ricochet neutrino experiment. His official group page explicitly states that he is looking for graduate and undergraduate students and offers third- and fourth-year supervised-course positions. This is the clearest immediate opportunity.

The initial message should propose a specific computational contribution, such as detector-response modelling, simulation validation, low-background data analysis, or statistical inference. It should ask whether a scoped PHY37x/PHY47x project is available this term and whether he expects to consider Fall 2027 graduate students.

### Priority 2 — Miriam Diamond

Prof. Diamond's SuperCDMS work includes data acquisition, data-quality management, low-mass dark-matter analysis, and detector simulations. This is a strong bridge from Bayesian inference and Python to experimental dark-matter research. Recruiting status is not publicly confirmed, so the message should ask about a supervised undergraduate project rather than assume an opening.

### Priority 3 — David Curtin

Prof. Curtin studies complex and atomic dark matter, dark galactic disks, dark sectors, and collider phenomenology. His group uses custom N-body, stellar-evolution, and gas-physics codes to connect dark-sector theories to astrophysical observations. This may be the most intellectually direct bridge between the MaNGA halo project and computational particle physics, but the email must show adequate theoretical preparation and refer to one current direction.

### Secondary — Yonatan Kahn

Prof. Kahn's group works on dark-matter direct detection, axions, collider/beam-dump searches, and theoretical machine learning. Contact is most useful if the UCL project's details or HY's advanced coursework make the theoretical fit concrete.

### Outreach rules

## 6. Recommended initial email structure

**Subject:** U of T Physics student interested in a supervised project on [specific topic]

1. Identify yourself as a fourth-year U of T Physics student and name the exact research direction from the faculty member's current page.
2. Describe the MaNGA Bayesian dark-matter project in one sentence, including PyMC 5/MCMC and the actual manuscript status.
3. Describe the UCL computational particle-physics project in one sentence, with the specific method and current result once available.
4. Propose one plausible task relevant to the group.
5. Ask whether the professor would be open to a 15–20 minute meeting about a PHY37x/PHY47x project this term and possible Fall 2027 graduate research fit.

The message should not claim that the MaNGA manuscript is published until an arXiv or journal record exists.

## 7. Statement of intent positioning

The central theme should remain:

> Use Bayesian inference and scientific computation to study dark matter across astrophysical and particle-physics settings.

Recommended structure:

1. Open with one research question about inferring dark matter from indirect and noisy signals.
2. Use the MaNGA project to demonstrate probabilistic modelling, uncertainty analysis, and independent research output.
3. Use the UCL project to demonstrate computational particle physics, close expert supervision, and current technical growth.
4. Add any Fall 2026 U of T supervised project as evidence that the proposed fit has already been tested in the local research environment.
5. Name two or three faculty members across a coherent primary field and one adjacent field. The department explicitly encourages applicants to consider a few subfields because supervision availability affects admission.
6. Explain why the one-year research-report/project MSc is the appropriate preparation for doctoral research.

Do not rely on “I already know U of T” as the school-specific argument. The department asks why its research environment, faculty, and program structure serve the proposed work.
