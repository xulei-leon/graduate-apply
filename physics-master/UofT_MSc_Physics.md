## Fit and difficulty

- **Match:** High — HY's Bayesian dark-matter halo work, scientific Python experience, and computational particle-physics project fit the department's dark-matter, experimental particle, theoretical high-energy, and affiliated astrophysics research.
- **Difficulty:** Reach under the currently known 3.0/4.0 cumulative GPA. The decisive unresolved number is the final-year or most relevant upper-year average: a B+ or stronger record makes the application substantially more viable, while a result below mid-B would miss the SGS Calendar minimum.
- **Main strengths:** two connected research projects, first-author manuscript preparation, Bayesian MCMC and Python, and the potential for a strong UCL research letter.
- **Main risks:** the cumulative GPA is near or below the published thresholds; the department says it receives many more qualified applications than it can admit; and only two reference letters are permitted.
- **Citizenship caveat:** a U of T bachelor's degree supports the English-test exemption but does not change domestic/international classification. The department says it admits only a small number of international applicants, so citizenship or permanent-resident status should be considered separately.

## U of T undergraduate status

No published rule prevents a current U of T Physics undergraduate from entering the U of T Physics MSc. The same minimum qualifications and committee review apply. The department says applications are adjudicated from the written application and supporting documents by the Graduate Admission Committee; it does not publish an internal-applicant preference or guaranteed pathway.

The practical advantage is access rather than an admissions bonus. A current student can build a recent, locally verifiable research record, take a supervised research course, attend seminars, and obtain a detailed letter from a U of T faculty member. The department's own FAQ says a letter from a professor who supervised a small final-year research project is more informative than one from the professor of a large first-year course.

For 2026–27, PHY37x/PHY47x supervised courses permit experimental, computational, or theoretical projects. Students arrange the course with a faculty supervisor, normally work 8–12 hours per week, meet regularly, submit a written report, and complete an oral examination. The normal minimum cumulative GPA is 2.7, and proposals should be submitted as soon as possible and no later than one week before the Faculty course-enrolment deadline.
