### Recommended contact order

1. Confirm with `grad.sec@astro.utoronto.ca` that external MSc applications will be accepted for Fall 2027.
2. Contact **Josh Speagle or Gwendolyn Eadie first**, not both with a generic message. The email should connect Research 1's halo-inference model, sampling choices, convergence work, and manuscript status with the exact ML method used within Research 2.
3. Contact **Ting Li** with a separate survey-and-dark-matter message focused on large astronomical datasets, stellar streams or dwarf galaxies.
4. Contact **Aviad Levis** only if the ML component of Research 2 used physics-informed ML, differentiable simulation, inverse methods, or a closely related approach. Otherwise keep him secondary.
5. Approach **Jo Bovy** only with a concrete Galactic-dynamics question and evidence that HY has read the relevant work or used `galpy`-adjacent methods.

The MSc requires two research courses with different supervisors. A coherent pairing would be **Eadie/Speagle/Levis for Bayesian or ML methodology** plus **Bovy/Li for Galactic dynamics or observational dark-matter constraints**. This is a planning direction, not a promise that any faculty member will supervise the applicant.

## Fit note

This is a much closer topical match than the Physics MSc for HY's MaNGA dark-matter halo inference project. It must nevertheless be treated as a separate, high-risk application rather than a second track inside Physics.

The machine-learning component of the UCL computational particle-physics project strengthens the application as evidence of transferable methodology—model construction, validation, uncertainty awareness, simulation or data-pipeline work—rather than as proof of prior astronomy specialization. The application should describe Research 2 as one coherent project and state the ML model, dataset or simulation, HY's individual contribution, evaluation method, and concrete output rather than relying on the label “machine learning.”
