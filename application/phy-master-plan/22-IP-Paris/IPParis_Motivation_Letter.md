# Motivation Letter for the Master Year 1 Physics

Hongyi Xu  
University of Toronto | hyi.xu@mail.utoronto.ca

Dear Members of the Admissions Committee,

I am applying to the Master Year 1 Physics at Institut Polytechnique de Paris for autumn 2027, with High Energy Physics (HEP) as my preferred major. I am completing an Honours Bachelor of Science in the Physics Specialist program at the University of Toronto, with graduation expected in June 2027. My research on galaxy kinematics and simulated Higgs events has drawn me towards computational physics, particularly how modelling assumptions and statistical methods affect physical inference.

In 2025–2026, I independently investigated the dark-matter halo concentration–mass relation using 620 selected MaNGA disk galaxies. I developed a rotation-curve mass-decomposition pipeline in Python and PyMC, connecting galaxy-level Markov chain Monte Carlo posteriors to population-level inference. Under fixed photometric inclinations, I recovered a modest negative slope; subsample tests allowing for inclination uncertainty broadened the constraints. This taught me to examine how modelling assumptions affect a physical conclusion, beyond whether a numerical fit converges. A first-author manuscript is complete but has not yet been submitted.

Since May 2026, I have been working remotely with Professor Mario Campanelli at University College London on neural-network event selection in simulated Higgs four-lepton events. Using public ATLAS simulated signal and four-lepton background samples, I construct kinematic variables from lepton four-momenta and compare classifiers trained on different input groups for the 2e2μ final state. My work involves separating training, validation and evaluation samples and examining how selection affects expected event yields and four-lepton mass distributions. I also use profile-likelihood analysis and bootstrap resampling to investigate statistical uncertainty. This ongoing project is developing my ability to connect computational choices with physical questions and to document and validate an analysis.

My undergraduate training in theoretical physics, laboratory work, and programming has provided a foundation for these projects. I am currently taking Computational Physics, Introduction to High Energy Physics, and Time Series Analysis. I now seek stronger theoretical and numerical training to choose and justify physical models more independently.

IP Paris's HEP major would help me build on this preparation. Physics of elementary particles would deepen the physical basis of my Higgs work. Among the electives, Numerical physics would extend my modelling skills, while Cosmology would connect my dark-matter halo research to the evolution of cosmic structure. I would discuss these choices with the coordinator to develop a coherent study plan.

The CMS team at Laboratoire Leprince-Ringuet is a particular attraction. Its study of Higgs properties through the four-lepton channel closely connects with my current work. Christophe Ochando's 2026 proposal on multi-lepton Higgs analyses and neural simulation-based inference points to methods I would like to learn: targeting physical parameters directly and validating the resulting uncertainty estimates. I would bring experience with ATLAS simulations while learning the experimental and analysis context of CMS.

Laboratory research projects and the four- to six-month internship would let me develop these interests through sustained collaboration. My long-term goal is a PhD in computational physics, developing reliable inference methods for fundamental-physics measurements. The M1 would help me pursue this goal with a stronger foundation in particle physics.

Yours sincerely,

Hongyi Xu
