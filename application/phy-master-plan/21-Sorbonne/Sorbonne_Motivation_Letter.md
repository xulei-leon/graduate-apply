# Motivation Letter for the Paris Physics Master

Hongyi Xu  
University of Toronto | hyi.xu@mail.utoronto.ca

Dear Members of the Admissions Committee,

I am applying to the M1 Paris Physics Master at Sorbonne Université for autumn 2027. I am completing an Honours Bachelor of Science in the Physics Specialist program at the University of Toronto and expect to graduate in June 2027. My research in galaxy kinematics and particle physics simulations has given me a clear motivation for further study: I want to understand what we can learn about physical systems through computational models, and how confidently we can draw those conclusions. This question drives my interest in statistical inference, Bayesian modelling, and machine learning for physics. The PPM's combination of fundamental physics, numerical training, and research experience is especially compelling to me as preparation for a research career.

My undergraduate training in classical mechanics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, laboratory work, and programming has provided the foundation for translating physical questions into computational analyses. In my research, I have put this training into practice by building models in Python and examining their parameter uncertainties, numerical stability, and underlying assumptions. I am now taking Computational Physics, Introduction to High Energy Physics, and Time Series Analysis, alongside further theoretical physics coursework, to deepen the knowledge on which this work depends.

In 2025–2026, I independently investigated the dark-matter halo concentration–mass relation using 620 selected MaNGA disk galaxies. I developed a rotation-curve mass-decomposition pipeline in Python and PyMC, using MCMC to retain correlated uncertainties in halo mass and concentration, and linked individual-galaxy posteriors to population-level inference through prior-corrected importance sampling. Under fixed photometric inclinations, I recovered a modest negative slope for the selected galaxies, while subsample tests showed that allowing for inclination uncertainty broadened the constraints. Working through this limitation taught me to distinguish numerical stability from the strength of a physical conclusion. It also deepened my interest in separating what galaxy kinematics reveal about halo structure from what depends on prior and model choices. Taking this project from analysis to a completed first-author manuscript has strengthened my ability to work independently and scrutinise my own results. The manuscript has not yet been submitted.

Since May 2026, I have worked remotely with Professor Mario Campanelli at University College London on kinematic feature selection for signal-strength inference in simulated Higgs four-lepton events. Using public ATLAS simulated signal and background samples, I compared MLP classifiers built from different feature groups and evaluated the resulting signal-strength constraints through a common pyhf profile-likelihood analysis. I used Shapley attribution, bootstrap resampling, and conditional coverage diagnostics to examine feature contributions and inference reliability. Classifier AUC and signal-strength interval width did not always favour the same inputs. This finding sharpened the question that most interests me in machine learning for physics: how should we evaluate a model in terms of the physical inference it supports? Investigating that question, while testing the reliability of apparent precision gains, has strengthened my motivation to pursue computational particle physics. A first-author manuscript is in preparation.

These projects have clarified both what I can contribute to the PPM and what I want to learn. I would bring experience in building computational analyses and critically assessing their results, together with a strong desire to deepen my understanding of the physics behind them. Advanced quantum mechanics and Statistical physics would strengthen my conceptual foundations, while Numerical methods for physics would extend my computational training beyond the approaches I have used so far. I am particularly drawn to the M1 courses in astrophysics and cosmology and in nuclear and particle physics, which would deepen my understanding of the physical systems studied in my two research projects. I also value the opportunity to connect computational analysis with measurement through laboratory training. The compulsory research internship is a central reason for my application: I am eager to help develop a research question within a team, contribute my modelling and diagnostic skills, and refine my scientific judgement through sustained discussion with researchers.

My long-term goal is to pursue a PhD in physics, using computational and statistical methods to investigate questions in particle physics or astrophysics. I see the PPM as a purposeful next step: its broad M1 training would help me make an informed choice of M2 specialisation and develop the judgement needed to formulate and investigate physical questions independently. I am eager to contribute the initiative and care I have brought to my research while learning from the programme's teachers and fellow students.

Yours sincerely,

Hongyi Xu
