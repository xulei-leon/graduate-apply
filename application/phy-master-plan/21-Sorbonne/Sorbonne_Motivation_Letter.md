# Motivation Letter for the Paris Physics Master

Hongyi Xu  
University of Toronto | hyi.xu@mail.utoronto.ca

Dear Members of the Admissions Committee,

I am applying for admission to the M1 Paris Physics Master at Sorbonne Université for autumn 2027. I am completing an Honours Bachelor of Science in the Physics Specialist program at the University of Toronto and expect to graduate in June 2027. My research interests lie in computational and high-energy physics, particularly statistical inference, Bayesian modelling, and machine learning for particle physics and astrophysics. Through research in galaxy kinematics and particle physics simulations, I have become interested in how these methods help us constrain physical models and assess the reliability of our conclusions. I want to develop these interests through the PPM's combination of fundamental physics, numerical training, and research experience.

My undergraduate training in classical mechanics, electromagnetism, quantum mechanics, laboratory work, and programming has prepared me to translate physical questions into computational analyses. Working with models and data in Python has taught me to examine parameter uncertainty, numerical stability, and the assumptions behind a result. I am currently taking Computational Physics, Introduction to High Energy Physics, and Time Series Analysis, alongside further theoretical physics coursework, to strengthen the foundations of my research.

In 2025–2026, I independently studied the dark-matter halo concentration–mass relation using 620 selected MaNGA disk galaxies. I developed a rotation-curve mass-decomposition pipeline in Python and PyMC, using MCMC to retain correlated uncertainties in halo mass and concentration. I then linked individual-galaxy posteriors to population-level inference through prior-corrected importance sampling. Under fixed photometric inclinations, I recovered a modest negative slope for the selected galaxies, but subsample tests showed that allowing for inclination uncertainty broadened the constraints. This taught me that a numerically stable estimate can still depend strongly on assumptions about the observations. It also sharpened my interest in distinguishing what the data tell us about halo structure from what depends on prior and model choices. My first-author manuscript is complete but has not yet been submitted.

Since May 2026, I have worked remotely with Professor Mario Campanelli at University College London on kinematic feature selection for signal-strength inference in simulated Higgs four-lepton events. Using public ATLAS simulated signal and background samples, I compared MLP classifiers built from different feature groups and used a common pyhf profile-likelihood analysis to evaluate the resulting signal-strength constraints. I used Shapley attribution, bootstrap resampling, and conditional coverage diagnostics to examine feature contributions and inference reliability. Classifier AUC and signal-strength interval width did not always favour the same inputs. This reinforced the importance of evaluating machine learning through the physical inference it supports, while checking whether apparent precision gains are reliable. A first-author manuscript is in preparation.

The PPM appeals to me because it combines the breadth of physics training I now seek with opportunities to develop these research interests. Advanced quantum mechanics and Statistical physics would deepen my conceptual foundations, while Numerical methods for physics would help me broaden my computational training beyond the approaches used in my projects. I am particularly interested in Astrophysics and cosmology and Nuclear and particle physics, which would strengthen my understanding of the physical systems underlying both studies. Laboratory training would also help me connect computational analysis more closely with measurement and experimental practice. Through the compulsory research internship, I hope to develop a question with a research team, examine the assumptions of an analysis, and learn from discussion with researchers throughout the work. My experience in modelling and diagnostic checks would provide a foundation for contributing to such a project.

My long-term goal is to pursue a PhD in physics, using computational and statistical methods to investigate questions in particle physics or astrophysics. The PPM would help me build a stronger basis for choosing a subsequent M2 specialisation and become more capable of formulating and investigating physical questions independently. I would welcome the opportunity to bring my experience in Bayesian modelling and machine learning to the programme while developing a broader understanding of physics.

Yours sincerely,

Hongyi Xu
