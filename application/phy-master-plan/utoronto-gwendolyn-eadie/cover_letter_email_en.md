Dear Professor Eadie,

I am a Physics Specialist undergraduate at the University of Toronto, expecting to graduate in June 2027 and exploring the Astronomy and Astrophysics MSc for Fall 2027. I am interested in your co-authored paper [Hierarchical Bayesian Inference of Globular Cluster Properties](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.03704), particularly its joint treatment of stellar measurement errors and the uncertain cluster centre. The distance–mass degeneracy illustrated in Figure 8 connects with my interest in propagating uncertainty rather than fixing poorly constrained quantities in dynamical inference.

During 2025–2026, I completed a MaNGA project on the halo concentration–mass relation independently. I learned PyMC Bayesian modelling and MCMC to address mass–concentration degeneracy in rotation-curve fits, then used prior-corrected importance sampling to infer the relation's slope, normalization and intrinsic scatter from joint posterior samples for 620 selected disk galaxies. Prior-sensitivity, inclination and PSIS checks helped me examine where constraints depended on modelling assumptions or unstable reweighting. I wrote a first-author manuscript, now complete but not submitted; a preview is available at https://hyi03.github.io/manga-dm/paper-preview.

I also work under Professor Mario Campanelli at UCL on simulated Higgs four-lepton events, using pyhf profile-likelihood inference, event-group bootstrap and conditional coverage diagnostics to examine finite-sample uncertainty and interval calibration. Results remain exploratory, and a first-author manuscript is in progress.

For an MSc research project, I would be interested in a simulated cluster example comparing mass inference with the cluster distance fixed versus inferred, while varying measurement errors. I would need to learn the relevant distribution-function model. Subject to the department accepting MSc applications for Fall 2027, might you consider supervising an MSc research project in this area, and what preparation would be most useful?

I would be happy to provide my CV and further research details. Thank you for your time.
