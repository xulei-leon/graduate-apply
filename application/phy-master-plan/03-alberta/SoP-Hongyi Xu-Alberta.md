# Statement of Purpose

I am applying to the MSc in Physics at the University of Alberta for Fall 2027. As a Physics Specialist student at the University of Toronto, expecting to graduate in June 2027, I have become particularly interested in computational physics and statistical inference through research on galaxy kinematics and simulated Higgs events. I now want to understand how astrophysical models and detector effects shape the interpretation of dark-matter searches.

In 2025–2026, I investigated the dark-matter halo concentration–mass relation using 620 selected MaNGA disk galaxies. I built a Python and PyMC pipeline to decompose galaxy rotation curves and estimate halo parameters using Markov chain Monte Carlo, accounting for correlations between halo mass and concentration. I then used prior-corrected importance sampling to connect individual-galaxy results to population-level inference. Sensitivity tests showed that the inferred relationship depended on modelling assumptions, particularly when inclination uncertainty was considered. This made me more attentive to the assumptions behind an apparently clear statistical result. I have completed a first-author manuscript based on this work, which has not yet been submitted.

Since May 2026, I have been working remotely with Professor Mario Campanelli at University College London on neural-network event selection in simulated Higgs four-lepton events. Using public ATLAS simulated signal and four-lepton background samples, I construct kinematic variables from lepton four-momenta and compare classifiers trained on different input groups for the 2e2μ final state. My work involves separating training, validation and evaluation samples and examining how selection affects expected event yields and four-lepton mass distributions. I also use profile-likelihood analysis and bootstrap resampling to investigate statistical uncertainty. This ongoing project is developing my ability to connect computational choices with physical questions and to document and validate an analysis.

My coursework in theoretical and experimental physics, together with programming and current studies in computational physics, high-energy physics, and time-series analysis, has prepared me for further research. At Alberta, I would like to build on my experience with uncertainty analysis and computational modelling while learning more about dark-matter detection, recoil kinematics, detector response, and background modelling. I am particularly interested in Professor Marie-Cécile Piro’s research on dark-matter detection, detector microphysics, and directional signatures. Her work connecting astrophysical dark-matter distributions with predicted recoil signals reflects the kind of question I would like to explore: how do assumptions about the underlying physics and the detector affect the constraints drawn from experimental data? With appropriate supervision, I would be interested in modelling directional recoil signals under different dark-matter velocity distributions and investigating how detector response and backgrounds influence the resulting constraints. My previous projects provide a useful starting point, although I would need to develop the relevant detection physics and simulation techniques. After the MSc, I intend to pursue doctoral research using computational and statistical methods to investigate fundamental physics. Alberta would give me the opportunity to strengthen my physical understanding, develop greater independence in research, and contribute my experience in Python-based analysis and uncertainty assessment to astroparticle physics.
