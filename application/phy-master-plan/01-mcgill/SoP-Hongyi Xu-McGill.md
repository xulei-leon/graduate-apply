# Statement of Purpose

I am applying to the Master of Science in Physics (Thesis) at McGill University for Fall 2027. I am completing an Honours Bachelor of Science in the Physics Specialist program at the University of Toronto and expect to graduate in June 2027. My research on galaxy kinematics and simulated Higgs events has drawn me towards computational physics and statistical inference. In both settings, I have become interested in how observations constrain physical models, and how those constraints change when we reconsider the assumptions behind an analysis.

In 2025–2026, I independently investigated the dark-matter halo concentration–mass relation using 620 selected MaNGA disk galaxies. I built a rotation-curve mass-decomposition pipeline in Python and PyMC, using Markov chain Monte Carlo to retain correlated uncertainties in halo mass and concentration. I connected the galaxy-level posteriors to population-level inference through prior-corrected importance sampling and examined the analysis through prior-sensitivity tests and sampling diagnostics. Under fixed photometric inclinations, I recovered a modest negative slope for the selected galaxies, but subsample tests allowing for inclination uncertainty broadened the constraints. This limitation made me more attentive to the distinction between a stable numerical result and a well-supported physical interpretation. The project has led to a completed first-author manuscript that has not yet been submitted.

Since May 2026, I have been working remotely with Professor Mario Campanelli at University College London on neural-network event selection in simulated Higgs four-lepton events. Using public ATLAS simulated signal and four-lepton background samples, I construct kinematic variables from lepton four-momenta and compare classifiers trained on different input groups for the 2e2μ final state. My work involves separating training, validation and evaluation samples and examining how selection affects expected event yields and four-lepton mass distributions. I also use profile-likelihood analysis and bootstrap resampling to investigate statistical uncertainty. This ongoing project is developing my ability to connect computational choices with physical questions and to document and validate an analysis.

My undergraduate training in theoretical physics, laboratory work, and programming has prepared me for further study, and my current coursework in computational physics, high-energy physics, and time-series analysis has strengthened my interest in computational and statistical approaches to fundamental physics. McGill’s combination of graduate coursework and sustained thesis research would allow me to deepen my theoretical understanding while developing greater independence in research. I am particularly interested in Professor James Cline’s work at the intersection of particle physics and cosmology, including dark-matter models and their astrophysical implications. Building on my MaNGA project, I would like to explore how uncertainties in inferred halo properties affect the evaluation of physical models of dark matter. I am also interested in Professor Brigitte Vachon’s work with the McGill ATLAS group. My research on simulated Higgs events has introduced me to statistical inference in collider physics, and I would welcome the opportunity to investigate how detector response and background modelling influence experimental constraints. McGill’s research across particle physics and cosmology would allow me to pursue these interests while learning from a research group and refining a focused MSc thesis question.

After graduation, I want to pursue a PhD in computational astrophysics or a related field. In the long term, I aspire to become a research scientist studying galaxy formation and evolution, using computational models and observational evidence to investigate the physical processes that shape the universe. At McGill, I hope to deepen my physical understanding, develop a clearer research direction, and contribute the care in modelling and uncertainty analysis that I have begun to develop through my undergraduate projects.
